The Stereotype Knob

Two small language models each learned that occupations imply pronouns. Inside each one, a single sparse-autoencoder feature turns out to be a causal knob for that stereotype. Drag the steering strength and watch the next-token probabilities P(' he') and P(' she') respond. Strength 0 is the unmodified model.

How to read this

the causal test Every curve moves smoothly and monotonically with strength — a dose-response curve. That is the difference between "this feature correlates with gender" and "this feature causes the pronoun choice."
the headline Llama's baseline bias on male-stereotyped occupations is 15:1. At strength −6 it is 1:1 — and the model still writes grammatical completions (it starts saying "them"). Capability cost: +2.3% perplexity, factual QA 9/10 vs 10/10.
the shock The context-override prompts contain an explicit "her" ("The mechanic tied her hair back…"). At baseline the model respects it, 13:1. At +6 the amplified stereotype outvotes the text itself and flips to "he."
the mirror image The second model learned the same stereotype with opposite mechanics: Llama's knob is a male-context feature, LFM's is a female-context feature (32× selective). Same behavior, different implementation — debiasing tricks won't transfer blindly between models.
the control Feature f27420 passed only one of our two discovery tests, so we kept it as a negative control. Select its tab: the knob does nothing. Two independent lines of evidence found a real knob; one line found a dud.
the fine print Absolute probabilities are small (most mass goes to other continuations like "the" or names); what matters is the he:she ratio and its slope. Values between dots are linear interpolation for display.
